## What the demo shows

Aomi tested the same requests in two setups: Claude executing on its own, and Claude using Aomi. The requests were:

- On Ethereum: Lido into Aave
- On Solana: Marinade into Kamino

Without Aomi, Claude ran into failed transactions, missing accounts, RPC limits and protocol-specific requirements.

With Aomi, it could read the relevant state, build transactions, simulate the full execution, and prepare a validated transaction for signing. The model interprets the intent. The wallet holds the key. Aomi handles the execution layer in between.

The post points people to [chat.aomi.dev](https://chat.aomi.dev) and [build.aomi.dev](https://build.aomi.dev).

## How to try a similar flow yourself

1. Open [chat.aomi.dev](https://chat.aomi.dev). Read-only questions do not need a wallet.
2. Connect a wallet or sign in. On EVM, ownership is proved with SIWE. On Solana, it is SIWS. Keys stay in your wallet.
3. Set the network for the thread, then ask for one of the same kinds of tasks in plain English, for example staking through Lido and supplying to Aave, or staking through Marinade and using Kamino.
4. Wait for Aomi to build and simulate the transaction on a fork of live state. Review the simulated outcome in the chat.
5. If you proceed, your wallet signs. If you reject the prompt, nothing happens onchain.

One limit matters for the Solana half: portal simulation covers EVM chains only. Solana requests are signed and sent without a fork simulation, so review the transaction details in your wallet.
