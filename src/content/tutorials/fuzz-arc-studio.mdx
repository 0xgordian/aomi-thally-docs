## What the video shows

Aomi's agentic fuzzer does stateful attack exploration on smart contracts. An agent proposes hypotheses. A deterministic campaign engine then executes ordered call sequences on an isolated fork. Only confirmed violations are reported.

The example is a small vault in Arc Studio. Intended behavior is that a deposit credits the caller, but withdraw transfers without checking that credit. The exploit depends on ordering: someone funds the vault, then a stranger drains it. In the official description, that is Alice depositing and Bob withdrawing her funds. The finding is reduced to two calls and checked twice.

The prototype connects to an Arc Studio workspace, reads compiled contracts, and lets Aomi explore attack sequences on an isolated Arc fork. Developers can review the execution trace and reproduction evidence.

## How to look at it

The video description gives the prototype URL and a requirement:

1. Open [arc-studio-fuzz.vercel.app](https://arc-studio-fuzz.vercel.app).
2. You need an Arc Studio token to connect.
3. Load a compiled contract from an Arc Studio workspace.
4. Review any confirmed finding, including the short reproduction sequence. Nothing in this workflow should sign or broadcast a transaction.

The Alice and Bob vault is the recording's example, not a specific live contract.
